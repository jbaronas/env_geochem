Write at least 10 words first.
%ET = 100 × qETqrain = 100 × (1 − ClrainClpw)

%ET: the share of the rain lost by evapotranspiration (%). qET: water lost by evapotranspiration (mm/yr). qrain: rain (mm/yr). Clrain: Cl concentration in rain (µmol/L). Clpw: Cl concentration in pore water, which is soil water (µmol/L). It comes from two budgets for the soil, as in the lecture. Water: qrain = qET + qpw. Cl: qrain × Clrain = qpw × Clpw, because no Cl is lost with the water lost by ET.

Worked example: %ET, for a different soil

Rain has 40 µmol/L of Cl, and a soil water has 160 µmol/L.

%ET = 100 × (1 − 40 µmol/L160 µmol/L) = 75 %: three quarters of the rain has been lost, and the Cl is four times higher.

Correcting for rain

Na* = Na − Cl × (Na/Cl)rain

Na*: Na corrected for rain inputs and ET (µmol/L). Na: Na concentration in the soil water (µmol/L). Cl: Cl concentration in the soil water (µmol/L). (Na/Cl)rain: the molar ratio in rain (mol/mol), from the rain row.

One step corrects for two things

The Cl in a soil water has already been concentrated by evapotranspiration, and so has the Na that came with the rain, by the same factor. So Cl × (Na/Cl)rain is the rain's Na after evapotranspiration, and subtracting it corrects for both the rain input and the concentration by ET.

This only works while nearly all of the Cl is from rain. Where Cl is also added by road salt, wastewater, fertiliser or evaporites (remember the road-salt plan in Week 4), the wrong amount of Na is subtracted.

Worked example: Na*, for a different soil

A soil water has 300 µmol/L of Na and 250 µmol/L of Cl. Rain has Na/Cl = 0.86 mol/mol.

  1. Na from rain = 250 µmol/L × 0.86 mol/mol = 215 µmol/L.
  2. Na* = 300 µmol/L − 215 µmol/L = 85 µmol/L.

The steps from the lecture
  1. Na* = Na − Cl × (Na/Cl)rain
  2. Ca from rain = Cl × (Ca/Cl)rain
  3. Casil = Na* × (Ca/Na)silicate rock
  4. Cacarb = Ca − Ca from rain − Casil

All concentrations are in µmol/L, and ratios are in mol/mol. Carbonates contain almost no Na, so all Na* is taken to be from silicates. (Ca/Na)silicate rock is in cell B13 of the sheet: 0.35 mol/mol (Gaillardet et al. 1999). Keep it in that one cell, and point your formula at it.

Glossary

TermMeaning
Evapotranspiration (ET)Water lost by evaporation and by transpiration through plants. Dissolved elements are not lost with it.
Conservative soluteOne that is neither added nor lost along a flow path. Cl is close to conservative.
Cyclic saltsSea salts carried inland by wind and added to the land with rain.
Pore waterWater held in the spaces between grains in a soil or rock.
Throughfall and stemflowRain after it has passed through a canopy, dripping from leaves or running down stems.
Major ionsThe most concentrated ions in natural waters: abundant in rock, and mobile.
Corrected for rain, X*The concentration of X after subtracting what was supplied by rain.
End-memberA source with a known, distinctive composition.
